## 1 Preface

The RoboMaster University Series (RMU, RoboMaster University Series) was initiated by DJI and is one of the events under the National Collegiate Robot Competition. It is a robot competition and academic exchange platform created for technology enthusiasts worldwide. Since its founding in 2013, it has always upheld the philosophy of "giving glory to youth, giving power to thought, and serving young engineers worldwide to become dreamers who pursue excellence and are action-oriented". It is committed to cultivating and attracting well-rounded talent with an engineering mindset, and to conveying the beauty of technology and the concept of technological innovation to the public at large.

The National Collegiate Robot Competition RoboMaster University Championship (RMUC, RoboMaster University Championship) focuses on assessing participants' comprehensive application of science and engineering disciplines and their engineering practice ability. It fully integrates many robot-related technical disciplines such as "mechanical engineering", "embedded system design", "control algorithms", "electrical engineering", "integrated communication and sensing", "autonomous navigation" and "human-computer interaction". It also innovatively combines popular presentation styles with robot competition, making robot combat more intuitive and intense and attracting wide attention from many technology enthusiasts and the general public.

## 2 Participation

### 2.1 Registration Requirements

A team may register if it meets any one of the following conditions:

- The team successfully passed the RMUC 2025 Mid-Term Progress assessment, or ranked within the top 104 in the RMUC 2026 Full-Form assessment (entered the video assessment stage).
- The team successfully advanced from the group stage of the RMUL 2026 3V3 competition; for non-Class-A teams, the condition may be relaxed to winning a second prize in the main awards.

> Note:
> - A combined team (joint team) must have at least one member team that meets the RMUC registration eligibility in order to register.
> - The RMUC registration conditions will be adjusted according to the actual situation of each season and determined when registration for that season opens.

### 2.2 Participating Teams

> Note:
> - Due to legal and regulatory restrictions overseas, teams from overseas regions and countries cannot yet create teams or register on the official RoboMaster platform. Team creation and registration, the subsequent Technical Assessment stages of the event, and the collection of personnel lists for overseas teams will all be conducted through Wenjuanxing (questionnaire platform). Overseas team registration entry: https://djistore.wjx.cn/vm/Q4bFVhT.aspx
> - The distinction between Mainland teams, Hong Kong/Macao/Taiwan teams and overseas teams is used only to explain cases where the participation process differs because of the different geographic locations of the universities.

Participating teams must follow these principles:

**R1** All participating teams must have the team created on the RoboMaster forum by the team's persons in charge (team captain, lead teacher). Teams that already exist do not need to be created again. When creating a team, team information must be supplemented as the team requires; the invitation code must be obtained by contacting RoboMaster event affairs. After the team is set up, the team's persons in charge can invite team members to join through an invitation link. For the specific registration process, see the registration guide. Registration succeeds if all requirements of this section are met. The RMOC will open the participant list adjustment function irregularly during the season, and teams may change their team members during the open period. All information is based on the list as last confirmed in the current open period; it cannot be modified after the open period is missed. Before each event is held, the registration center will open one last time, and the RMOC will then use the finally submitted information to produce and issue award certificates.

**R2** The same team must use the same team name when taking part in different events. The team name must take the form "XXX Team" (战队), where "XXX" is a name defined by the team. The custom part of the name must not exceed 16 characters (each Chinese character counts as 2 characters, each English letter counts as 1 character), and must not contain the school name, an abbreviation of the school name, an English abbreviation of the school name, words such as "队", "团队" or "战队", or special symbols such as "*/-+". The team name must reflect the team's proactive spirit and comply with national laws and regulations. If the RMOC determines that a team name does not fit the spirit of the competition, it has the right to require the team to change it.

> Note: After a team submits its registration materials, the team name may not be modified.

<!-- p.8 -->
**R3** A team must be based at one institution of higher education, and must meet the personnel role, headcount and identity requirements specified in "2.3 Participants".

**R4** In principle, within the same event (challenge), only one team from a school is eligible to participate. If a school's campuses are not in the same city or in similar situations, so that interested students cannot form a team together, the RMOC may, after verification, allow the school to field teams from different campuses. If more than one team from a school registers, the specific registration review rules are given in the "RoboMaster 2027 University Series Representative Team List Announcement". Registrants must ensure that registration information is complete and accurate and bear the corresponding responsibility; registrants bear all consequences of missing or incorrect registration information. In special circumstances, contact the RMOC to explain, and the RMOC will handle the case according to the situation. The RMOC reserves the right of final interpretation.

> Note: If a team fails to meet any of R1-R4, the RMOC will reject its registration application. The team may resubmit after revising it to meet the requirements.

**R5** Two schools that do not each have a separate team may form a combined team across schools.

- A. Before a combined team is established, the schools concerned must form it only after fully communicating and planning the team in light of their own circumstances. The operating and R&D costs, personnel allocation and any other disputes arising from the combined team are to be handled and resolved by the schools concerned themselves; the RMOC bears no responsibility.
- B. Once a combined team is established, it may not be split, and it may take part in this season's RMUC only under that combined-team identity. If a combined team that has passed the registration review is split, it is regarded as voluntarily giving up its participation eligibility.
- C. The name of a combined team ends with "联队" (combined team) instead of "战队". The combined team must upload to the registration system a combined-team declaration letter issued by the schools of the participating teams. The template of the declaration letter is given in the registration system step description.

**R6** Any participant may take part in only one team throughout this season.

**R7** If teams do not satisfy any one of the "five differences" principle, they are regarded as the same team.

> Note: The "five differences" principle: different team names, different team members, different advisors, different affiliated organizations (school units such as colleges), and different participating robots.

**R8** Provided that the registration requirements of the relevant events are met, one team may register at the same time for different events of the RoboMaster University Series (including RMUC and RMUL).

**R9** The RMOC regards the same team taking part in different events in the same season as a single whole and handles all event affairs (including material donation, material purchase, participation support, etc.) uniformly. After season registration ends, the whole cannot be split.

## 4 Technical Assessment

The Technical Assessment lets teams demonstrate their technical level, guides reasonable preparation planning and the team's inheritance and development, and trains participants' comprehensive abilities in requirements analysis, cost budgeting, data analysis and report writing. All teams must complete the corresponding Technical Assessment stages within the specified time ranges according to the RMOC's requirements, take the initiative, and fully demonstrate the team's strength.

RMUC 2027 mainly includes six Technical Assessment stages: Season Planning, Rules Test, Mid-Term Progress Document, Referee System Assessment, Full-Form Assessment and Season Summary. The latest tasks and requirements of each Technical Assessment stage are subject to forum announcements. For the schedule of each stage, see "3 Season Schedule".

The submission standards for tables and videos in each Technical Assessment stage are as follows:

> Note: Tables and videos must be attached to the forum report of each stage as attachments.

**Table 4-1 Submission Content Standards**

| Document Type | Standard |
| --- | --- |

| Video | - Resolution: higher than 720P<br>- Upload method: upload the video to the report attachments<br>- Content:<br>  - Include subtitles or placards explaining every process in the video<br>  - Show only effective content; keep the video pace compact and within the specified duration |

### 4.1 Season Planning

Number of submissions: 1
Pass condition: Obtain a grade of "C" or above
Submission content and requirements:

- Planning document: RoboMaster forum report text, no word limit (use concise language)
- Schedule: Excel document
- Team budget: Excel document

### 4.2 Rules Test

The Rules Test covers: the "RoboMaster 2027 University Series Robot Building Specifications Manual", the "RoboMaster 2027 RMUC Rules Manual" and related competition provisions.

### 4.3 Mid-Term Progress Document

Number of submissions: 1
Pass condition:

- Project document grade of "C" or above

Submission content and requirements:

- Schedule (updated): Excel document
- Project analysis: RoboMaster forum report text

### 4.4 Referee System Assessment

The Referee System Assessment content mainly includes: the "RoboMaster 2027 University Series Robot Building Specifications Manual", the "RoboMaster Referee System User Manual", the Referee System FAQ, the Referee System onboard module manuals, and other content on the installation standards, usage and common problem handling of the onboard Referee System modules. The Referee System Assessment is conducted as an online video assessment, with each team sending the specified number of members to take part. A team that completes the assessment as required and meets the pass standard is regarded as having passed the Referee System Assessment stage.

- Technical plan: RoboMaster forum report text

  - Cost report: RoboMaster forum report text
  - Expenditure ledger: Excel document
  - Project analysis: RoboMaster forum report text
  - Robot type BOM: Excel document

### 4.6 Season Summary

Number of submissions: 1
Pass condition: Obtain a grade of "C" or above
Submission content and requirements:

Summary document: RoboMaster forum report text

### 5.3 Open-Source Awards

Teams applying for Open-Source Awards must open-source their core technologies or operations and management methods through channels such as the RoboMaster forum during the 2027 season (May to August 2027), in order to promote the development of the RoboMaster competition and to promote engineering culture and spirit.

The Open-Source Awards have no fixed number of winners. ### Appendix Competition Safety Notice

All RoboMaster participants must fully understand that safety is the most important prerequisite for the sustained development of the competition. To protect the rights and interests of all participants and the event organizers, and in accordance with relevant laws and regulations, by registering for the RoboMaster competition all participants acknowledge and abide by the following safety terms:

1. All participants must ensure that they have full capacity for civil conduct and the ability to build and operate robots independently, and must ensure that, before building robots with products of the event organizer, SZ DJI Technology Co., Ltd., they have carefully read the registration notice, competition rules and other related documents.
2. During the event, ensure that the building, testing and use of all robots will not cause injury to teammates or opposing team members, referees, staff, spectators, equipment or the competition venue.
3. Ensure that the robot structure design takes into account the convenience of the robot safety inspection during Pre-Match Inspection, and actively cooperate with the organizer's Pre-Match Inspection.
4. The use of fuel-powered engines, explosives and hazardous chemical materials is prohibited, as is the use of hydraulic or other power methods that may cause pollution.
5. At any time during R&D, preparation and competition, team members must pay full attention to safety and comply with the *RoboMaster University Series Battery Safety Specifications*; advisors must take responsibility for safety guidance and supervision.
6. Ensure the safety of the robots, ensure that their launching mechanisms are in a safe state, and ensure that at no time will they directly or indirectly injure team members, referees, staff or spectators.
7. During R&D, training and competition, take sufficient and necessary safety measures against possible accidents, for example: wear protective goggles; avoid loss of control of the control system; urge team members to think through operation steps in advance to avoid mis-operation and collisions between team members and between team members and robots; strictly prohibit team members from training alone, to ensure someone can respond to an accident in an emergency; when debugging, appropriate safety measures such as locking and adding an emergency stop switch must be applied in the robot system.
8. For all accident liability and corresponding losses arising during practice and competition from robot malfunction, unmanned aerial vehicle loss of control in flight, or similar accidents, the team that caused the accident is solely responsible.
9. Each team must strictly abide by local laws and regulations on flight control. Flights by a team's Aerial Robot above specific restricted areas of the competition field in the venue are indoor flights and do not involve airspace approval. To ensure the flight safety of the Aerial Robot, it is connected to a ground fixing stake by a rope. If an accident occurs, such as the Aerial Robot breaking free of the safety rope, the pilot must land and stop the propellers as soon as possible, and continued flight is strictly prohibited. Flying a team's Aerial Robot outdoors is strictly prohibited.
10. Items sold and provided by the event organizer, SZ DJI Technology Co., Ltd., such as batteries and the Referee System, must be used according to the instruction documents. If improper use causes harm to anyone, SZ DJI Technology Co., Ltd. bears no responsibility. Personal injury and property loss to the team itself or any third party caused by building or operating robots are borne by the team.

11. Strictly comply with national or regional laws, regulations and related provisions; ensure that robots are used only for RoboMaster-related activities and competitions, are not illegally modified, and are not used for any other illegal purpose.
