Full-field mapping divergence — 2026-10-22

Historical evidence. Live commitments: software/sim, physical validation. Draft/open status below records the dated checkpoint, not a separate maintained action list. Summary

The head-mounted lidar violated slam_toolbox's rigid-mount assumption; tracking base lidar instead of root fixes that. AMCL caches the mount position too (follow-up). A separate Gazebo VelocityControl artifact sustains chassis tilt and lifts the robot after rough crossings, and continuous planar scan insertion then corrupts the map. Pausing insertion during transfers and observing from level, parked stations gave a 48.7 m field map with 97.55% reverse coverage within 0.15 m. Hardware competition readiness is not established by this sim fixture. No robot movement, firmware, production route, noise model, matcher threshold or accuracy gate changed.

Moving-mount failure

A head-sweeping lidar breaks slam_toolbox when the base is root.
slam_toolbox 2.8.5 getLaser() caches a laser by scan frame and LaserAssistant sets one offset relative to the base, but root->lidar changes with the head (about 0.22 m lever arm). Refreshing TF alone does not refresh Karto's stored offset.
Switching the SLAM base to lidar removes the error on a controlled circuit.
Same 8.956 m circuit, 0.30 m/s, raw odometry, head sweeping about ±1 rad; only the base changed. The root-base run had truth roll/pitch below 0.002 rad yet invented 0.583 rad of map-root yaw and reached 0.946 m error.

With base_frame: lidar the cached offset is identity and each stamped nav_odom->lidar lookup carries head translation and yaw. SLAM still publishes map->nav_odom; the public pose, AMCL and rf2o keep the root frame. The angular travel threshold now measures lidar/head rotation. Physical tilt and height absent from the planar TF tree remain uncorrected. The bug is hardware-relevant (head slew alone reproduced it on flat ground) and is not explained by simulated slip or raised regions.

Terrain and the artificial climb

With the frame fix, continuous insertion on the broad route still corrupts the map.
broad-lidar-raw (48.565 m) recovered after crossings but had 0.4608 m p95 / 1.1013 m max mapping error and spurious cells. Truth reached z=0.6091 m, roll=0.7106 rad. Raw/navigation position stayed within 0.265 micrometres of truth, ruling out wheel drift; all 2,410 scans were well formed (720 beams, endpoint error below 0.4 microradians), distinct from the malformed Oct 18 hardware scan stream.
The sustained climb is a simulator artifact, not a robot behaviour.
Gazebo 8.15.0 VelocityControl sets velocity every update and the Physics system rotates the body command through the full chassis orientation, so zero body z gives nonzero world z when tilted. At t=194–204 s (roll 0.1394 rad) the model predicts measured vertical speed with 0.00000436 m/s RMS residual (mean commanded 0.0411, observed 0.0313 m/s). The root rose about 0.23 to 0.48 m with over 1 m clearance: powered free-space motion, not evidence hardware would climb.
Slowing down does not fix it.
At 0.15 m/s broad-lidar-slow still lifted and eventually failed the unchanged no-progress guard. Stopping only removes the commanded vertical component; the fixture then falls about 1 cm/s.
Neither base selection nor a wider search window can recover the missing observations.
Tilted scans hit the floor and an elevated lidar sees over obstacles in the nominal-height map; the emulator discards chassis roll, pitch and height. Correcting the fixture belongs to simulation movement, outside this task. Real rough-ground scan tilt remains an independent hardware risk.

AMCL moving mount

nav2_amcl has a partial form of the SLAM bug.
addNewScanner caches the first-scan root->lidar x/y and never refreshes it; updateFilter re-reads only mount yaw. With the lidar 0.22 m off the head axis, a head turn of Δ moves the true origin up to 2·0.22·sin(Δ/2) m from the cached one.
Parked scoring cannot detect it; driving does.
Without odometry motion AMCL adds no motion noise: forced parked updates with 2.0 rad head travel still scored 0.05 mm p95. Table below.
The fix is TrackedMountAmcl in localization_backend.
It sets the laser pose from root->lidar at the scan stamp before AMCL's callback; AMCL keeps root as base so seeds, /amcl_pose and convergence gates are unchanged. All runs have 100% stamped TF coverage and return within 0.035 m; tracked-mount parked p95 was 0.02–0.03 mm. Switching AMCL's base to lidar/head would also work but changes seed yaw semantics.

Input default

rf2o/EKF fails the level-station drive; the workflow now defaults to raw wheel input.
Cached mount: rf2o/EKF drive 0.7599 / 1.0881 m (fail) vs raw 0.0712 / 0.0846 m; parked 0.0288 vs 0.00003 m p95. With tracked mount and head sweep rf2o/EKF still failed (0.1342 m p95, 0.5211 m max), so it is not the cache bug (rf2o's fork already refreshes its mount per scan). --rf2o is opt-in. Sim raw odometry is exact, so this comparison favours raw; on the robot slip goes uncorrected in nav_odom and is left to SLAM/AMCL. Why rf2o/EKF fails on this route is not yet isolated.

Mapping pause

operator_workflow pause|resume SESSION replaces the hand-run toggle: it reads paused_new_measurements, toggles only when needed and verifies afterwards. Live against slam_toolbox in a sim room session each call produced the requested state, repeats left it unchanged, and a mismatched session was refused without toggling.

Hardware risks

  • Fixed scan geometry, true lidar zero/blind sector, extrinsics, surveyed heading, wheel slip, time synchronization and dynamic obstacles remain.
  • Pausing insertion needs enough wheel odometry and overlapping views to reconnect at the next station; this fixture's exact raw input does not establish that margin. Filter convergence is not measured field accuracy.
  • AMCL's tracked mount depends on accurate, timely head TF: a stale or wrong head angle now moves the modelled sensor origin.
  • Real rough-ground tilt is unmodelled in SLAM and AMCL alike.
