Observed failure and cause

The first scored hosted mcb_drive run failed startup on a lifecycle service response, not slow map I/O.
AMCL logged its last initialization 12.9 ms after configure began (map read took 3.0 ms), then could not send its /amcl/change_state response. The manager never activated the nodes and E2E exhausted its 30 s map-to-root wait. This is the same failure class as the reported map_server stall.
The cause is the service-response writer timing out before it discovers the client's response reader.
rmw_fastrtps 8.4.4 source returns a timeout without sending when the reader has not matched by the blocking deadline; request-direction discovery does not imply response-direction readiness. The error came 105.7 ms after AMCL's last initialization log.
Moving the race to another remote startup service was not enough.
A rejected dynamic-composition trial hit the same error on /localization_container/_container/load_node (1/20 startups). It had odom->root at 9.95 s and nav_odom->odom at 9.98 s but no active backend or map transform by 40 s.
No persistent missing raw root TF was reproduced.
Raw root was present in every hosted startup probe. The harness's full map->root lookup conflated missing backend activation with missing raw TF. No replacement or fallback TF publisher was added.

Fix

The localization owner now builds each backend and its lifecycle manager in one C++ process.
One executor per node, backend nodes exist before the manager starts, lifecycle endpoints share a DDS participant and no remote component-loading RPC remains. Node names, YAML scopes, initial pose and deadlines are preserved; no retries or timeout changes. An intermediate plugin-factory loader had clean startups but reproducible shutdown SIGSEGVs and was rejected. The final backend had zero abnormal shutdowns over its smoke run and 20 startup trials.
The sim harness no longer restarts a failed shared-sim robot startup.
It fails after its existing single 30 s wait; errors distinguish the raw, navigation and map links. The old automatic retry masked a broken startup and was not an acceptable reliability mechanism.

Clock transport follow-up, 2026-10-17

The orchestrator accepted the pooled drive comparison; remaining gates were two full 9/9 drift suites and three drive confirmations after diagnosing the clock stall.

Clock wait and transport evidence

The stall is a blocked global clock publisher, not an EKF wakeup bug.
"Waiting for clock to start" appears in all nine scenarios of earlier nightly cohorts and is normal. 100 EKF starts with a plain publisher, 100 with the Gazebo bridge and 1,000 in-process waits all initialized. A full fixed-backend diagnostic reproduced it: five scenarios passed, four failed startup, /clock froze at 202.512 s (age grew to 162 s), EKF sat in wait_until_started and the bridge's Gazebo callback was blocked in SharedMemTransport::push_discard. Its mutex lies in the mapping of a deleted /dev/shm/fastrtps_port41943.
What deleted the SHM port and the exact interleaving are not established.
Fast DDS 2.14.6 takes the port's empty_cv_mutex in try_push. The deleting process was not captured; deletion is not attributed to logind, a cleanup script or another container.
The fatal stall was captured only with the fixed backend, never on nightly.
A full nightly diagnostic passed 9/9 (max clock age 0.854 s). The bridge and SHM transport are the same on both backends, and a backend-free comparison shows nightly already exposes the SHM path. This is treated as a common transport vulnerability exposed by new launch timing; it is not an observed nightly stall, and a timing effect of composition is not ruled out.

Fix and focused check

Sim fcb998e pins only the clock bridge to the existing thornbots_pkg/config/fastdds_udp_only.xml. Discovery peers, /clock QoS, timestamps, EKF/backend settings, timeouts and no-retry policy are unchanged. The registered C++ test_clock_bridge runs without a world or backend, starts five fresh contexts, requires the clock to start and advance, that no Fast DDS SHM ports are mapped, and clean shutdown. The original transport receives time but fails the SHM invariant; the fix passes. This checks the removed path without relying on a stochastic deadlock.
